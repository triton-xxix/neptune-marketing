The 10-Point Checklist

Work through these steps in order. Each builds on the last to create a systematic lead reactivation machine.

1

Segment Your Dormant Leads by Project Type

Don't treat all old leads the same. Group them by the service they enquired about: extensions, loft conversions, kitchen renovations, bathroom refits, or general renovations. Each segment needs different messaging and timing.

  • High-ticket projects (extensions, conversions) need longer nurture sequences
  • Seasonal projects (garden work, exterior painting) can be reactivated at optimal times
  • Emergency repairs can be contacted immediately regardless of dormancy
2

Calculate Your Dormant Lead Value

Before starting, know your numbers. Count your dormant leads and multiply by your average project value. If you have 500 old leads and your average job is £15,000, you're sitting on £7.5M in potential pipeline.

3

Clean Your Contact Data

Remove duplicates, update phone numbers where possible, and verify email addresses. A clean database improves deliverability and response rates. Tools like NeverBounce or ZeroBounce can validate emails in bulk.

4

Choose Your Reactivation Channel

Home improvement leads respond best to personal, direct outreach:

  • SMS: 90%+ open rates, best for short, personal messages
  • Email: Good for sharing portfolio work and detailed offers
  • Phone: Highest conversion but most time-intensive
  • WhatsApp: Growing preference for sharing photos and project ideas
5

Craft the "Soft Touch" Opening Message

Never open with "Are you still interested?" That's salesy and creates resistance. Instead, lead with value or relevance:

  • "Hi [Name], we just completed a similar [project type] to what you were considering. Thought you might like to see the before/after photos."
  • "Hi [Name], with spring approaching, we're booking [service] projects for March. Wanted to reach out in case you're still considering it."
6

Build a 5-Touch Sequence (Not Just One Message)

One message isn't enough. Create a sequence spaced over 3-4 weeks:

  • Touch 1: Soft value-based opener
  • Touch 2: Social proof (recent project photos/reviews)
  • Touch 3: Seasonal relevance or special offer
  • Touch 4: Direct question about their project timeline
  • Touch 5: Final "break-up" message (gives them permission to say no)
7

Use Social Proof That Matches Their Project

Generic testimonials don't work. Show them a project similar to what they wanted — same type of work, similar property, or local to their area. Before/after photos are your most powerful reactivation tool.

8

Create a Low-Friction Offer

Don't ask for a commitment on the first contact. Offer something easy:

  • Free site visit with updated quote
  • 15-minute phone consultation to discuss their plans
  • Portfolio of similar completed projects
  • Financing options or payment plans
9

Track Responses and Adjust

Monitor open rates, reply rates, and conversion to appointments. Test different message angles:

  • Which project type segments respond best?
  • What time of day gets the best reply rate?
  • Which offers get the most bookings?
10

Systematize for Ongoing Reactivation

Don't make this a one-time campaign. Build a system:

  • Auto-tag new leads that go 30 days without booking
  • Schedule quarterly reactivation campaigns
  • Create templates for your best-performing messages
  • Train a team member to handle responses
